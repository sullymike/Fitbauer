# Fitbauer versus NORMOS coverage: full inventory

*English version of `COBERTURA_NORMOS.md`. The Spanish file is the original;
this one is kept in step with it.*

**2026-08-02.** Closing of the review of the NORMOS Fortran source
(SITE + DIST, 1990) against `core/`. Unlike the validation bank —which compares
RESULTS over spectra generated by the binary— this is an inventory of
CAPABILITIES: the complete list of parameters and switches was extracted from
the namelists (`sitemdos.for`, `distmdos.for`) and cross-checked one by one
against what Fitbauer does.

Verdict symbols:

- **=** does the same (verified)
- **+** does the same and is more accurate
- **~** covers it partially or by another route
- **✗** does not have it

## 5. Where Fitbauer is measurably better

| | NORMOS | Fitbauer |
|---|---|---|
| Hamiltonian diagonalisation | **general** complex EISPACK in `REAL*4`, with `MACHEP=2⁻⁴⁷` (double-precision epsilon) and `IERR` never checked | Hermitian LAPACK in double; energies exact to 1.8·10⁻¹⁵ |
| Source kernel (transmission) | point sampling of the Lorentzian | integrated per channel; model rms 2.8·10⁻³ → 1.5·10⁻⁴ |
| Voigt profile | approximate pseudo-Voigt | exact Voigt (`wofz`) |
| Sub-channel folding | truncates to a whole channel (misaligns ≤½ channel) | cubic interpolation |
| Linked-parameter error | **bug**: uses the offset instead of the factor → zero with a multiplicative constraint | correct propagation |
| Insufficient-grid diagnostic | — | `edge_pileup` |

## 6. What is missing, by practical relevance in ⁵⁷Fe

The two gaps with experimental meaning —P(δ) and the Brand model— were CLOSED
on 2026-08-02; see §8. What remains is developed in
**`PENDING_NORMOS_EN.md`**, with the exact source reference, what to touch in
Fitbauer, how to validate it and whether it is worth it. Summary:

1. **Analytical Czjzek / Le Caër** (`DISTRI=4`). The histogram reproduces the
   shape (validated in series L2), but there is no 2-3 parameter closed form to
   fit directly.
2. **`BEXT`**, external field in Ising relaxation: it shifts the lines as well
   as polarizing the populations. The polarization is in place; the shift is
   not.
3. **Emission spectra** (`EMSPEC`). A sign change in the model, but not
   exposed.
4. **Other isotopes** (¹¹⁹Sn, ¹⁹⁷Au, ¹⁵¹Eu, ¹²¹Sb). Fitbauer is ⁵⁷Fe.
5. **Preprocessing**: `NADD` (rebin), `NDECKS` (add spectra), `MULT`/`ADD`.
6. **M1+E2 multipole mixing** (`PHS`/`MIX`). No effect in ⁵⁷Fe.

## 7. Convention differences worth knowing

None of these is an error; all are covered by a parameter or documented.

- **Sextet line positions**: SITE derives them from the nuclear moments;
  Fitbauer uses the published α-Fe pattern. They do not differ by a simple
  scale factor. Selectable with `sextet_pattern("normos")`.
- **Intensities**: `D13`/`D23` are AREA ratios; `int1`/`int2` are DEPTH ratios.
  They agree when the widths are equal. Selectable with
  `intensity_convention("area")`.
- **Line widths**: `WID` is that of lines 3,4 and `W13`/`W23` are relative to
  it; `gamma1` is that of lines 1,6.
- **Relaxation**: `k = OME/2`, and NORMOS splits the width between `WD`
  (eigenvalues) and `WDS` (convolution) while here there is only one.
- **χ²**: NORMOS divides by the data with DF = NP−1−NVAR.
- **Folding point**: the `PFP` in `&DATA` is only the SEED of the search, not
  the final point; NORMOS refines it over two cycles. And the continuous point
  it prints in the `.RES` is not where it folds either: the final routine
  (`normospr.for:601-604`) TRUNCATES it and adds whole channels, with the axis
  at `⌊PFP⌋ + 0.5` (`core.normos_job.punto_de_doblado_normos`). Fitbauer does
  interpolate, so its centre need not coincide.
- **DIST grid**: the subspectra are `x_k = X + (k−1)·DTX`, `k = 1..NSB`, with
  `X`/`DTX` = `BHF`/`DTB`, `QUA`/`DTQ` or `ISO`/`DTI` depending on `METHOD`.
  The origin and the step are FITTABLE PARAMETERS (`BHFFIT`, `DTBFIT`…), so the
  grid in the `.RES` need not be the one in the `.JOB`.
- **`DTQ` in field distributions**: the `distcalf.for` loops for METHOD 1-5
  compute `RH = BHF+PP*DTB` and `RI = ISO+PP*DTI` and **do not touch ΔEQ**. A
  `DTQ` written into a field `.JOB` does nothing; it only enters in METHOD 6/7,
  where the grid is already a quadrupole one. Carrying it over introduced a
  correlation that never existed (with `DTQ=0.03` and 40 points, a ΔEQ moving
  more than 1 mm/s).
- **`λ` versus `alpha`**: NORMOS's `LAMDA` is absolute and multiplies `D₂ᵀD₂`
  over unnormalised counts; Fitbauer's `alpha` is dimensionless and normalised
  by `λ_ref`. **They are not 1:1 transferable** — `alpha` has to be set with
  the L-curve. The `BETA/LAMDA` RATIO is preserved and becomes the
  `edge_anchor`.
- **`DEX` versus `ARX`**: the 1994 binary renamed `ARX`→`DEX` just as it
  renamed `ARE`→`DEP` in SITE; it is still the resonant AREA in mm/s.
- **DIST namelist**: it does not accept `NLINE`, `DEP`, `W13`, `W23`, `AKS`,
  `IFTRAN`… If they are copied over from a SITE `.JOB`, **NORMOS reads and
  discards them without a word**: that subspectrum never entered the fit.
  Fitbauer's importer does say so.

## 8. Closing the two gaps with experimental meaning (2026-08-02)

### P(δ) — isomer shift distribution

**The engine already knew how to do it**: `build_kernel` had accepted
`variable="delta"` all along. What was missing was exposing it — the CLI
blocked it with `choices=["bhf","quad"]` — and having it documented anywhere.
Verified end to end on a synthetic bimodal P(δ) of singlets (the exact case of
`METHOD=8`): ⟨δ⟩ = 0.468 versus 0.470 and σ = 0.372 versus 0.369, separating
the two populations (0.15 and 0.85 mm/s).

Now `--variable delta`, with a default range of −1…2 mm/s and its own labels.
It goes **beyond** NORMOS: their `METHOD=8` distributes singlets only, whereas
here the kernel can be a singlet, a doublet or a sextet (δ distributed with a
fixed field).

### The Brand model — widths from physical dispersions

New `width_model="dispersion"`: instead of leaving `gamma2`/`gamma3` free, the
width of each line follows from how much each dispersed variable shifts it,

    (Γ_i/2)² = (Γ/2)² + (∂v_i/∂B · σ_B)² + σ_δ² + (∂v_i/∂ΔE_Q · σ_Q)²

which is NORMOS's sum in quadrature. It reproduces the signature of chemical
disorder: with σ_B = 1 T lines 1 and 6 go to 0.408 mm/s while 3 and 4 stay at
0.255, whereas σ_δ broadens all of them equally.

A detail that matters: the broadening **conserves the area** of each line (a
dispersion spreads the same atoms around), so the peak weight drops in
proportion. Without that, the area grew by 40 % with σ_B = 1 T.

Brand's line **asymmetry** is not replicated. Their own code warns that the
expression "is only valid if the asymmetric terms are much smaller than the
width, and otherwise the full expression (`METHOD=3`) should be used"; for that
regime Fitbauer has the full histogram, which approximates nothing.
